How to Play

1. LOBBY — Customers arrive with engineering design problems. Click a customer to read their requirements, then choose a material to start their order.

2. INVENTORY & CLICK-TO-MOVE — Orders stay in your bottom tray. You can Drag & Drop OR Click an item in the tray, then click its destination (Furnace, Anvil, or Customer).

3. MELT STATION — Move a raw item into the furnace. Click and HOLD the "🔥 Hold to Heat" button to raise the temperature. When it hits the green target, click "✅ Take Out".

4. FORGE STATION — Move a heated item onto the anvil. The left panel shows live design stats as you adjust thickness (1–10 mm). Green ✅ = constraint met. Once happy, hammer 5 times in the fast green zone!

5. HEAT TREATMENT — After forging, choose a cooling method. Quenching boosts strength but kills ductility. Annealing maximises ductility but weakens the part.

6. DELIVERY — Go back to the Lobby. Move the finished item to the customer to deliver and get graded!

📖 Engineering Handbook

⏸️ Game paused while you read — close to resume your shift.

Yield Strength (σy) [MPa]: Maximum stress before permanent deformation. Scales with thickness — thicker cross-section → higher load capacity.

Weight (Area Density) [kg/m²]: = material density × thickness. Light materials + thin gauge = lowest weight.

Cost [$]: = material unit cost × thickness. Expensive materials at thin gauge can still undercut cheap ones at thick gauge.

Ductility (Fracture Strain ε): A material property — NOT affected by thickness. ε > 0.05 = ductile; ε ≤ 0.05 = brittle.

Thermal Limit [°C]: Maximum service temperature. A material property — NOT affected by thickness.

Heat Treatments

  • Furnace Cool (Anneal): Relieves internal stresses. Boosts ductility, lowers strength.
  • Air Cool (Normalize): Returns part to baseline material properties.
  • Water Quench: Forms Martensite in steels → massive strength increase but becomes brittle!
