# Advancing STEM Education via AI-Generated Games

## Overview

Background: Lawrence Livermore National Laboratory (LLNL) is a Department of Energy laboratory that applies science and technology to national security, energy, and environmental challenges, and has a strong commitment to enhancing the future STEM workforce through educational outreach and academic engagement. LLNL has been developing smartphone-sensor-based STEM investigations and exploring AI-assisted workflows for analyzing the complex data these experiments produce.

Problem(s): Artificial intelligence offers extraordinary opportunities to advance learning in STEM by making computing more accessible for solving complex problems. However, there is also a significant risk of offloading critical thinking to AI and skipping the productive struggle that is central to long-term learning. Identifying strategies that use AI to enhance learning—without replacing reasoning—is an urgent challenge for educators and students.

### Goals

Design, build, validate, and evaluate a series of STEM learning games in which AI supports game creation and iteration, while the games themselves are designed to promote conceptual understanding and reasoning. The games will include real-time access to the sensors in smartphones and provide active feedback. The games will be hosted in a responsive web app providing selection of a growing series of mini-games. The broader goal is to demonstrate a “learning-by-building” approach, where developing and refining such games can be integrated into STEM courses as a mechanism for learning new topics. The idea is to create a number of games that can be run from a central web application (game center). The mentor is interested in seeing how the process of building the games helps the students better understand the “science” concept associated with each game. The mentor is also thinking about using game creation and evaluation as a new approach for students to learn about different STEM topics.
