Yield Strength (σ_y): The amount of stress (in MPa) a material can withstand before it plastically (permanently) deforms. Essential for load-bearing parts!

Fracture Strain (ε): A measure of ductility. Materials with ε > 0.05 are ductile (they bend before breaking). Materials with ε ≤ 0.05 are brittle (they shatter).

Heat Treatments (Cooling Rates)

The speed at which you cool a forged part alters its crystalline microstructure, drastically changing its properties!

  • Furnace Cool (Anneal): Very slow cooling. Relieves internal stresses, maximizing Ductility (ε), but lowers Yield Strength.
  • Air Cool (Normalize): Moderate cooling in ambient air. Returns the metal to its balanced, baseline properties.
  • Water Quench: Rapid cooling. In Carbon Steels, this traps carbon atoms, forming extremely hard Martensite. It massively increases Yield Strength but makes the part dangerously brittle!
